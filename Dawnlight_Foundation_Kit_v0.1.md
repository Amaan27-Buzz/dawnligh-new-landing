# DAWNLIGHT
## Foundation, Vision & Brand System
**Version 0.1 — 25 October 2026**
**Classification: Internal master / founder working document**

> Technology built around people.

This is the operating foundation for Dawnlight: what it exists to do, what it will and will not become, how it communicates, how its identity behaves, and what must be decided before publishing or building. It consolidates founder direction discussed to date; it is not a claim that the company, equity arrangement, research, or brand assets have completed legal or technical review.

**Read this first:** [LOCKED] indicates a founder-approved direction; [SELECTED] means chosen for exploration or current use but not necessarily validated; [PROPOSED] is recommended implementation guidance; [OPEN] needs a decision or audit. The newest research-first positioning supersedes older product-studio and local-first descriptions.

# 01 / The foundation

## One-sentence definition [LOCKED]
Dawnlight Labs is an independent research-focused technology laboratory exploring artificial intelligence, computing architectures, intelligent systems, human-computer interaction, and emerging hardware, while building useful technologies that bring those ideas closer to people.

## Purpose [LOCKED]
**The user matters.** This is the internal north star, not an obligation to use the phrase as a homepage headline. Research matters because it can expand what is possible; products matter because they turn capability into human benefit.

## Public positioning line [LOCKED]
**Technology built around people.**

The phrase must be backed by products that are easier to understand, useful, efficient, respectful of people’s resources, and honest about their limits. It does not mean every project must feature AI; it does not mean all technology must run locally.

## Mission [WORKING COPY]
Research new approaches to intelligence and computing, then turn the most useful ideas into technologies people can understand, control, and genuinely want to use.

## Vision [WORKING COPY]
A world in which intelligent technology is trusted, approachable, and genuinely valued—not because it claims to be advanced, but because it improves what people can do.

## Ambition [LOCKED DIRECTION]
Develop original architectures, models, systems, frameworks and potentially hardware—not merely applications assembled on top of existing platforms. Over the long term, Dawnlight may grow into a broader group with Dawnlight Labs as its research and technology arm. That is a direction, not a present-day corporate structure.

## What success means [LOCKED]
The ultimate measure is whether people **care that Dawnlight exists**—whether they value, trust, recommend and anticipate what it creates. Sustainable revenue is necessary; love and trust cannot be purchased with growth metrics.

## What failure means [LOCKED]
Profitability at the expense of users; intentionally degraded products; research theatre presented as achievement; a company recognizable only for hype, complexity or extraction.

# 02 / Strategic thesis & positioning

## Why this laboratory exists
AI product markets are increasingly crowded, and building another generic wrapper is not a sufficient durable research strategy. Dawnlight’s chosen emphasis is to investigate original computing approaches and make meaningful technical advances where it can. This is an ambition and allocation of attention, **not a guarantee of breakthroughs**.

## Two complementary engines [LOCKED]
**Research engine:** systematic exploration of models, architectures, memory, efficiency, edge intelligence, human-computer interaction, computing and hardware. This may involve original code, careful benchmarks, negative results, prototypes, collaborations and eventually publications.

**Product engine:** focused applications, open-source tools and experiments that solve real problems. These are useful in their own right, and they can also teach the laboratory about real constraints—deployment, usability, performance, reliability and user trust.

**Relationship:** research is the central long-term identity; products are a significant route to use and learning, not a distraction or a mandatory commercialization channel for every idea.

## What Dawnlight is / is not
| We are | We are not |
|---|---|
| An independent, developing research-focused technology organization | An established large institute with implied facilities or published breakthroughs |
| Interested in original models, architectures, software and hardware | Exclusively an AI chatbot or voice-software company |
| Committed to useful, accessible experiences | A collection of generic API wrappers marketed as inventions |
| Open to local, cloud or hybrid execution when appropriate | Local-first as a company-wide dogma |
| Selectively open source | Committed to publishing all research, IP or product code |
| Ambitious and experimental | Entitled to claim success without evidence |

## Who we serve [LOCKED]
Consumers, developers, creators, businesses and researchers may have different needs, but **paying more does not entitle a customer to greater respect**. The hierarchy of access, features or support can vary honestly with a product’s economics; the baseline dignity and usability cannot.

## Differentiation to earn, not claim [PROPOSED]
Original technical work; design refinement; practical efficiency; understandable products; unusually respectful defaults; clear communication of limitations; reproducible public evidence when disclosure is approved. Avoid making any of these unsupported comparative claims on the website.

# 03 / Research charter

## Research domains permitted in public copy [LOCKED]
- Artificial intelligence and machine learning
- Model and computing architectures
- Efficient, small-scale and on-device intelligence
- Multimodal and agentic systems
- Human-computer interaction
- Hardware–software systems and emerging computing technologies
- Experimental approaches to learning, memory, reasoning and execution (only at high level)

These are **areas of investigation**, not a portfolio of established findings.

## Research operating principles [PROPOSED]
- **Novelty with evidence:** state the question, baseline, method, experiment, limitation and outcome before describing an advance.
- **Reproducibility where possible:** record versions, data provenance, hardware, evaluation splits, seeds and failure cases internally.
- **Negative results count:** unsuccessful attempts can save time and refine the research direction.
- **Efficiency as a research variable:** report compute, memory, latency, energy or deployment complexity alongside quality when relevant.
- **Ethics at design time:** privacy, consent, bias, misuse, security and human control are considered before a public demonstration.
- **No theatre:** do not manufacture a research institute aesthetic to imply achievements not yet obtained.

## Disclosure policy [LOCKED]
The default classification for **unpatented, unpublished, unreviewed or otherwise confidential research is INTERNAL**. A generic domain can be named publicly; specific approaches, experimental pipelines, datasets, unreleased benchmark results, model weights, code, prompts, patentable methods, papers in preparation and unpublished architecture diagrams should not be published without an explicit review.

## Three-tier disclosure model [PROPOSED]
| Tier | Examples | Default action |
|---|---|---|
| Public domains | “Efficient AI”, “computing architectures”, “hardware–software systems” | Can appear on homepage |
| Public-approved project facts | OSCAR’s broad user-facing goal; Takokit’s general use; permitted screenshots | Release after factual/rights check |
| Internal research / potential IP | Model design, experiments, measurements, unpublished manuscripts, novel methods | Never publish by default |

## Pre-publication gate [PROPOSED]
1. Identify the owner of the material and whether all contributors consent.
2. Check invention/IP or patent-filing implications with qualified counsel where appropriate.
3. Check source, code, datasets, licenses, third-party assets and privacy implications.
4. Check every numerical claim against reproducible evidence.
5. Verify that images, demos and diagrams cannot expose internal tooling or secrets.
6. Record the approved version, scope, date and person authorizing publication.
7. If uncertain, publish the broad field and omit the technical claim.

## Publication stance [LOCKED DIRECTION]
Publish meaningful research when ready and cleared; collaborate where appropriate; retain the right to keep proprietary work private. Neither secrecy nor openness is an automatic virtue in every case.

**Status:** Active project; public release and feature-level availability must be checked before adding download CTAs.

**Public one-liner:** Turn plain-language intentions into commands you can inspect and control.

**What can be said:** AiSH helps users plan terminal operations in natural language while keeping commands visible and execution subject to user approval or control.

**Status:** Active project; inspect current release state before representing commands as safe, autonomous or available on a particular platform.

**Page narrative:** Intent → visible plan → review → controlled execution. A simulated example must be clearly labelled.

# 06 / Product ethics & economics

## The user's resources are real [LOCKED]
A product consumes more than money. It spends users’ **time, attention, compute, energy, memory, bandwidth, learning effort, data and trust**. Optimization and efficiency are core engineering and design principles because those resources matter.

## Non-negotiables [LOCKED]
- Never sell personal user data.
- Separate, explicit and informed consent before using user data for model training. No hidden consent, prechecked choices or pressure through degraded experience.
- No dark patterns or intentionally difficult cancellation.
- No artificial degradation to push upgrades, renewals or subscriptions.
- Do not knowingly create a problem merely to charge users to remove it.
- Avoid gimmicky AI features where ordinary software does the job better.
- Do not force local/cloud execution based on ideology; choose local, cloud or hybrid based on user benefit and actual constraints.
- Do not claim a product is ready when critical workflows do not work.
- Prefer human augmentation to unnecessary replacement.
- Provide clear pricing, limits, capability differences and privacy behaviour.

## Commercial model [LOCKED DIRECTION]
Free, paid, subscription, licence, support, sponsor or mixed models are chosen per product. Paid and open-source products should not include advertisements. Voluntary sponsorship for open-source work is acceptable. A product may be sold if it is mature and the terms are right; the founders’ expressed intent is not to sell Dawnlight itself. These are strategy preferences, not a substitute for legal agreements.

## Honest tiering [LOCKED]
Different tiers can have different capabilities or usage limits. Every tier must remain genuinely usable for its stated purpose. **Limit capability honestly; do not degrade experience artificially.** All customers receive baseline respect regardless of spend.

## Respectful failure modes [PROPOSED]
State what failed, why if known, whether work/data was saved, what can be retried, and what the user controls. Errors should not shame the user or pretend a backend failure was caused by them.

# 08 / Verbal identity & copy standards

## Primary public sentence [LOCKED]
**Technology built around people.**

## Internal principle [LOCKED]
**The user matters.** Use as a decision test. It is not automatically the best hero headline.

## Writing model [LOCKED]
Write plainly; be specific; sound human; be technically accurate; avoid self-congratulation; explain complexity without distorting it; use detail when it is useful. A sentence must communicate something even when stripped of its typography or animation.

## Avoid by default [LOCKED DIRECTION]
“Revolutionizing”, “seamless”, “unlock”, “game-changing”, “supercharge”, “next-generation”, “cutting-edge”, “the future of X”, “AI-powered solution”, “limitless”, “one platform for everything”, and empty statements about “transforming the world.” These are not banned words; they require evidence and context.

## Approved copy hierarchy [PROPOSED]
- **Identity headline:** express a position with economy.
- **Supporting copy:** establish what Dawnlight actually investigates and builds.
- **Research copy:** name domains and questions, never results not approved for disclosure.
- **Project copy:** say what the project is and what it lets someone do; state maturity.
- **Interface copy:** tell the user what happens next and what the consequences are.
- **Technical copy:** be precise, reproducible and unambiguous.

## Homepage copy starting point [WORKING COPY]
**Headline:** Technology built around people.

**Intro:** Dawnlight Labs is an independent research laboratory exploring artificial intelligence, computing and the technologies that connect them to everyday life.

**Research transition:** We investigate better ways to build intelligent systems—from new computing approaches to efficient systems and hardware.

**Projects transition:** Research is only one part of our work. We also make tools that solve problems people already have.

**Closing:** Curious about the work? Explore the projects or get in touch.

These are editable starting sentences, not compulsory final website copy.

## Specificity test
| Weak | Better |
|---|---|
| “The future of intelligent interaction” | “A personal intelligence designed to work across your devices.” |
| “A revolutionary voice ecosystem” | “Run and manage voice models on your own machine.” |
| “Empowering developers with AI” | “Review the commands before your shell runs them.” |
| “Groundbreaking research” | “We investigate new approaches to efficient computing.” |

## Microcopy examples [PROPOSED]
**Unreleased project:** “In development — public access is not available yet.”

**Demo safety:** “Preview only. No command will be executed.”

**Consent:** “Allow use of these selected recordings for model training? This is optional. Declining will not change access to the product.”

**Download:** “Available for Windows” only when the release has been verified; otherwise say “View project”.

- Do not replace its internal curved rim with a generic glossy sphere.

## Role is not a command to use all four [LOCKED DESIGN PRINCIPLE]
A page may use one or two font voices if that is enough. Mono is used where fixed-width information benefits from it—not on every uppercase label to signal technology. Light weight is not automatically appropriate for body copy at small sizes.

## Typography decisions must be optical [LOCKED]
Choose weight, tracking, line height, x-height, width and line breaks against real content at the final viewport. Do not blindly impose a Tailwind-style type scale, fixed letter spacing or a generic 64px centered hero. Evaluate baseline alignment, glyph confusion, punctuation, wrapping and texture at 12–18px.

## Starting hierarchy, not immutable tokens [PROPOSED]

| Hero display | 48–112px responsive | Authored line breaks; strong focus |
| Section heading | 30–58px | Composed against available width |
| Lead | 19–27px | Readable and concise |
| Body | 15–18px | Evaluate apparent size in Neuhade |
| Labels/navigation | 13–16px | Avoid excessive tracking |
| Technical data | 12–15px | Stable glyph distinction / tabular figures |

## Evaluation matrix [PROPOSED]
Test each candidate in long body copy; mobile navigation; multi-line hero; settings controls; research writing; buttons; table numbers; user-input fields; error copy; `Il1 O0 rn/m`; English and any planned supported languages; zoom at 200%; and light/dark themes. No role is considered production-valid until it survives these tests.

# 12 / Minimalism, composition & shape

## Definition [LOCKED]
**Minimalism is deliberate removal of anything that does not improve understanding, feeling or function.** It is not a preset combination of neutral sans-serif typography, enormous whitespace, rounded cards and muted gradients.

## Composition [LOCKED]
Design pages as coherent compositions, not assembled cards. Spacing, alignment, baselines, line length, section relationships, language and visual rhythm matter more than decorative effects. A mathematically regular grid can be optically corrected when there is a clear visual reason.

## Rules of restraint [LOCKED]
- No font selected merely because it signifies “tech”, “AI”, “luxury” or “editorial”.
- No card unless its boundary communicates grouping, interaction or meaningful distinction.
- No accent colour because a region feels empty.
- No animation because a section entered the viewport.
- No giant spacer as a substitute for page composition.
- No fake numerical proof, institutional authority, awards or endorsements.
- No unnecessary hiding of explanations, controls or important status behind icon-only interactions.

## Shape language [SELECTED DIRECTION]
Clean structured geometry with selective softness and moments of character. Mostly restrained rectangles, rules and compositional anchors; curves where they help touch, affordance, or the orb’s identity. Avoid a fixed “every container is a 24px card” style. Mild futurism can live in the mark, movement or lettering without making the site feel like sci-fi.

## Background and media [PROPOSED]
Editorial reading surfaces should be calm. Use genuine product screenshots, carefully labelled prototypes, original technical diagrams and real photography when available. Decorative 3D work should have a subject and reason. Do not invent researchers, labs, server farms or hardware photographs to imply facilities.

# 13 / Motion & interaction

## Motion hierarchy [LOCKED]
| Surface | Motion character |
|---|---|
| Corporate homepage | Calm, editorial, highly polished; motion sparingly used |
| Project detail pages | Expressive, technical or cinematic when explaining the project |
| Launch/campaign pages | Greater cinematic freedom |
| Product interfaces | Fast physical feedback and occasional character |
| Repeated workflows | Near-invisible motion; no animation tax |
| Brand film / announcement | Full narrative and cinematic freedom |

## The orb as a motion system [PROPOSED]
Orb interactions can use light, parallax, subtle rotation or reveal mechanics, as long as they are intentional, non-blocking, performant and capable of a still-state fallback. The secondary sun may be a memorable reveal or episodic transition, not a looping distraction.

## Motion quality gate [PROPOSED]
Motion earns its place by explaining cause/effect, preserving spatial context, confirming an action, demonstrating function, or creating a distinctive but restrained brand moment. Reject animation that causes layout shift, blocks reading, slows repeated actions, obscures content, traps interaction or makes motion-sensitive users uncomfortable.

## Accessibility [STANDARD]
Provide `prefers-reduced-motion` alternatives. Keep focus/keyboard interactions functional. Avoid autoplay audio. Never make core comprehension dependent on 3D, animation or hover. Support low-performance devices and progressively enhance expensive graphics.

# 16 / Internal operating standards

## Decision-making filters [PROPOSED]
Before a product, publication or design decision, ask:
1. What human problem or knowledge gap does this address?
2. What evidence supports the proposed approach?
3. What will it cost in money, attention, compute, complexity and trust?
4. Can the same result be achieved with simpler or more efficient means?
5. Are privacy, consent, permission and reversibility handled honestly?
6. Is the claim accurate at the project's present level of maturity?
7. Is this genuinely Dawnlight—or merely a recognizable industry fashion?

## Product stage labels [PROPOSED]
